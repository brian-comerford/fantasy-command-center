What the badges mean

Every callout you'll see next to a player, explained -- and what's actually behind the number.

Strong / Mixed / Split

Strong Mixed Split

Only appears when you've set up the optional Worker proxy (see the README's Worker section), which pulls in ESPN's and FFToday's own independent weekly projections alongside Sleeper's. Every number this app actually ranks, sorts, and totals with is still Sleeper's own projection, since these are Sleeper leagues and that's the number that determines real scoring. The other sources' opinions don't get averaged in for that purpose -- they're second/third opinions, and this badge shows how much they actually agree with Sleeper (and each other, when both are available):

  • Strong -- the sources are within 15% of their average. They basically agree; treat the number with confidence.
  • Mixed -- 15-35% apart. Real disagreement between the sources -- worth a second look.
  • Split -- more than 35% apart. The sources see this player very differently.

A small, muted "(blend N)" next to the projection is the average across whichever sources have data for that player -- purely informational, never what the app itself decides anything from. It's a per-player signal, not a head-to-head one -- so on a swap or waiver comparison, both players can show their own badge and blend note (each with its own tooltip), not just the one being recommended. Both are absent entirely for a K or DEF: ESPN blending skips those positions outright, and FFToday's kicker numbers aren't distance-bucketed while it has no real defense projections at all (rank only), so neither counts toward the blend for those two.

Hover (or tap, on mobile) the badge to see each source's exact number.

A Projections: Sleeper / Blend toggle sits above "Your current lineup" and "This week's opponent" when blend data's available -- it switches those two grids (and their totals) between Sleeper's own numbers and the blend, letting you compare side by side. It only affects those two grids: swap suggestions, waiver targets, and trade values always stay on plain Sleeper valuation regardless of which mode you're viewing. A player already actual-scored isn't affected either -- only projections for whoever hasn't played yet change.

CBS agrees / CBS disagrees

CBS agrees CBS disagrees

CBS Sports publishes its own consensus rank per position -- always on, no setup needed. It's a rank (e.g. "WR #14"), not a point value, so it's never blended into the projection itself -- it's a separate, third-opinion check. This tag compares CBS's rank for the two players in a suggested swap or waiver pickup, only when CBS has ranked both at the same position: agrees means CBS's board also favors the suggested player; disagrees means CBS would actually keep the other one.

Hover (or tap, on mobile) the tag to see both players' exact CBS rank.

Trending

Trending

This free agent is among the players most-added across all of Sleeper (not just your leagues) recently. It's a "the crowd is moving on this player" signal, separate from and not a substitute for the projection numbers next to it.

"Last wk X · Season avg Y (N gms)"

Actual scoring history, shown under the projection for comparison -- not a projection itself. Updates per player as soon as their own game concludes (a Thursday game shows up by Friday, without waiting on the rest of the week). The season average only counts weeks that player actually played: a bye week or a game missed to injury doesn't drag it down with a false zero, the way naively averaging every week would. Early in a new season, before a given player has played their first game yet, this falls back to last season's numbers instead -- labeled with the year (e.g. "2025 last game 12.4") so it's never mistaken for a current-season number.

Stats tab

Once Sleeper has posted real stats for a week (usually by Thursday night's game), it shows up here: your actual score for that week straight from Sleeper's own scoring, next to what your actual starting lineup was projected to score going in. The season cards above the week-by-week table roll all of that up -- record, points per week, best/worst week, and how often you've outscored your own projection.

The most recent week is left out of that season roll-up while it's still genuinely in progress -- a game or two left to play can still swing a live score by a lot. Once every game for that week has actually finished, it folds into the season cards automatically, even if Sleeper itself hasn't yet rolled its own "current week" forward to the next one (which it can take a day or two to do after a week wraps).

Good matchup / Tough matchup

Good matchup Tough matchup

How many fantasy points this player's actual opponent this week has allowed to their position all season, computed from real box scores (the same actual-stats data everything else on this page is built from) -- not a subjective guess. Good matchup means that defense is in the top third of the league at allowing points to that position; Tough matchup means the bottom third. The unremarkable middle third gets no badge, same as CBS agree/disagree above -- only a real signal is worth a callout. Needs at least one fully-completed week of stats league-wide, so there's nothing to show in the first week or so of a season.

Hover (or tap, on mobile) the badge to see the exact rank and points-per-game.

Usage ↑ / Usage ↓

Usage ↑ Usage ↓

This player's touches (targets plus rush attempts) in their most recent game, compared with their average over the games before that this season -- a leading indicator a single box score doesn't show, since a shrinking or expanding role often predicts next week's score better than last week's points did. Only shows up for a meaningful swing (roughly 20% or more, and at least one extra touch, in either direction); a steady role gets no badge. Needs at least two played games this season for that specific player, so it takes a couple of weeks to kick in for everyone -- earlier than that for anyone who missed time or just joined a roster.

Hover (or tap, on mobile) the badge to see the exact touches and snap share behind it.

Team needs / High need · Moderate need · Deep

WR · High need RB · Moderate need TE · Deep

The Waivers tab ranks your own players at each position against every player any team in your league has rostered there -- not just against your own single weakest player, which every roster has one of regardless of how deep it actually is. A position where your players sit near the top of that league-wide list is Deep; one where they're buried in the bottom half, or you don't have enough of them to fill your own starting need, is High need. This is what "3 good RBs but only 1-2 good WRs" looks like in practice, and it's why the pickup suggestions below are sorted by this instead of raw point edge alone -- a smaller edge at a position you're genuinely thin at can outrank a bigger one at a position you're already stacked.

Hover (or tap, on mobile) the badge on an individual pickup card to see the exact need score behind it.

+N.N VOR (Best value on waivers)

The Waivers tab's "Best value" list ranks free agents by value over replacement -- how many points above a replacement-level player at their own position they project, rather than by raw points, which would just always favor high-scoring positions like RB/WR over e.g. TE regardless of how replaceable a given player actually is. This list is independent of your own roster's needs above -- it's the strongest players still sitting on the wire, full stop.

Why a waiver or trade number sometimes isn't a projection

Waiver pickups and trade values normally use Sleeper's own projection for the week, same as everywhere else in this app. But a projection is a pre-game guess -- once that game's actually been played, the guess is just wrong, not merely out of date. Sleeper doesn't retroactively fix a finished week's projection, and rolling its own projections forward to the next week can lag a day or two behind the games themselves finishing. So on the Waivers and Trade tabs specifically (not the Lineup tab, which already shows real scores for your own starters as their games end -- see "Last wk X" above), any player whose game for the week is already over shows their real score here too, instead of a stale pre-game number. This self-corrects the moment Sleeper moves on to the next week's real projections -- there's nothing to turn on or off.

Injury Watch tags

Questionable (Knee)

The status and body part come straight from Sleeper's own injury report. "Bench option" and "Waiver option" are this app's suggested replacement if that player can't go -- the bench one accounts for how removing them reshuffles your other flex slots, not just a naive same-position swap; the waiver one is the best free agent at their position. Either can be missing if there's genuinely nothing better available.

Lineup lock heads-up

Shown on the Lineup tab whenever one of your current starters has an earlier kickoff than the rest of the week's games -- almost always a Thursday-nighter -- so it doesn't sneak past you while you're still planning around Sunday. Only looks at starters who haven't played yet, so it naturally moves on to the next-earliest kickoff once that one's underway, and disappears entirely once everyone in your lineup has played.

Upcoming bye weeks

Every rostered player's bye week, grouped and sorted, for the current week and beyond -- so a bye doesn't surprise you the week of, and you've got a heads-up to work the waiver wire or a trade beforehand instead of scrambling. A week highlighted in gold is this week or next.

No eligible RB available this week -- consider a waiver add before then.

A week highlighted in red goes a step further: it checks whether that week's byes actually leave a starting slot with nobody eligible to fill it at all, not just thinner than usual -- comparing your full roster's best lineup against the same lineup with that week's bye players pulled out. Only a slot that would've been filled at full strength and isn't anymore counts; a spot the roster was already light at regardless of byes doesn't trigger it.

When it does, two fixes are suggested for a single-position slot (QB/RB/WR/TE/K/DEF) -- a 1-for-1 swap (drop the gapped player outright for a free agent who isn't themselves on bye that week) or a temp fill-in (keep the gapped player, temporarily cut your least valuable bench spot instead, and reverse both moves once the bye week's over). Neither move happens automatically -- this app doesn't touch your roster -- and each comes with the same Ask Claude research button as any other suggestion. A FLEX-type gap (several positions' worth of bench out at once) is rare and ambiguous enough about which position to fix that it's flagged without a suggested swap.
